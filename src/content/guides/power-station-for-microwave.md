A microwave is one of the hardest common appliances for a portable battery — not because it uses much energy, but because it pulls a lot of power at once. Choosing a power station to run a microwave is all about **output watts**.

## Why a "1,000 W" microwave needs about 1,500 W

The wattage printed on the front of a microwave is its **cooking power** — the energy that reaches your food. Making microwaves isn't perfectly efficient, so the appliance draws more from the wall:

| Microwave cooking power | Typical draw from the outlet |
|---|---|
| 600–700 W (compact) | about 1,000–1,100 W |
| 900–1,000 W (standard) | about 1,400–1,600 W |
| 1,100–1,200 W (large) | about 1,600–1,800 W |

Your microwave's label (inside the door frame or on the back) lists the **input** watts or amps. Multiply amps by 120 to get watts: 13 A × 120 V = 1,560 W.

## The output you need

Your power station's **continuous output must be higher than the microwave's input watts**, with some headroom. For a standard 1,000 W microwave, look for **at least about 1,800 W** of continuous output. If the output is too low, the power station will shut off as soon as you press start.

## Energy per meal is small

Because you only run a microwave for a few minutes, it uses surprisingly little energy:

- **5 minutes at 1,500 W:** 1,500 × 5 ÷ 60 = **125 Wh**, or about **150 Wh** from the battery after inverter losses.
- **A day of light use (15 minutes):** about **375 Wh**, or about **440 Wh** from the battery.

On a full battery, total cooking time is roughly *capacity × 0.85 ÷ 1,500 W*:

- **1,000 Wh:** about 34 minutes of microwave time.
- **2,000 Wh:** about 68 minutes.
- **3,600 Wh:** about 2 hours.

## Using a microwave alongside a fridge

During an outage, plan your loads so the microwave and other big appliances don't run at the same moment. A fridge compressor starting while the microwave is on can add over 1,000 W of surge on top. If your power station is near its limit, unplug the fridge for the few minutes you cook.

## Alternatives that are easier on the battery

- **Electric kettle:** also about 1,500 W, but boils water in a few minutes for instant meals and drinks.
- **Compact 600–700 W microwave:** fits within the output of more mid-size power stations.
- **Gas camp stove outdoors:** uses no battery at all — never use it indoors because of carbon monoxide.
