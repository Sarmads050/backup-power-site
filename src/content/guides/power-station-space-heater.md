Can a power station run a space heater? Technically, yes — but probably not the way you're hoping. Heaters are some of the hungriest appliances in any home, and a battery empties fast when it's turning electricity straight into heat. Here are the real numbers, and smarter ways to stay warm.

## Why space heaters drain batteries so fast

Most plug-in space heaters draw about **1,500 W on high** and about **750 W on low**. They convert electricity directly into heat, and heat takes a lot of energy. For comparison, a whole day of LED lights, Wi-Fi and phone charging uses less energy than an hour of space heater on high.

There's also a hard limit: your power station's **continuous output must be above 1,500 W** to run a heater on high at all. Many small and mid-size units can't.

## How long a power station will run a heater

Runtime is roughly *capacity × 0.85 ÷ heater watts*:

| Power station | Heater on high (1,500 W) | Heater on low (750 W) |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 Wh | about 34 minutes | about 68 minutes |
| 2,000 Wh | about 68 minutes | about 2.3 hours |
| 3,600 Wh | about 2 hours | about 4 hours |

A heater's thermostat switches it on and off, which stretches these times somewhat — but even a large power station won't heat a room through a winter night.

## Heat yourself, not the room

The battery-friendly answer is to warm your body instead of the air:

- **Electric blanket or heated mattress pad:** about **50–100 W**. A 100 W blanket for 8 hours uses about **800 Wh**, so a 1,000 Wh power station can usually cover a full night.
- **Heated clothing and vests:** run from small USB or 12 V batteries for hours.
- **Hot water bottle:** boil water once (an electric kettle uses only a few minutes of power) and keep warm for hours.

Compared with a space heater, an electric blanket gets roughly **15 times the runtime** from the same battery.

## Keep the heat you already have

- **Shrink your space.** Close off unused rooms and gather in one small room, ideally one with few windows.
- **Block drafts.** Hang blankets over windows and doorways, and roll towels along the bottom of doors.
- **Layer up.** Hats, socks and layers keep in far more heat than a single heavy coat.
- **Use cold-rated sleeping bags** for the whole family at night.
- **Warm drinks and food** help your body make its own heat.

## What about your furnace?

If you have a **gas furnace**, the burner uses gas but the **blower fan needs electricity** — often around **800 W running** for a typical 1/2 HP blower, with a higher start-up surge. A large power station may be able to run it, and that heats your whole home far more efficiently than a space heater.

However, a furnace is hardwired. Connecting it to backup power requires a **licensed electrician and a transfer switch** — never try to backfeed your home's wiring.

## Safety: what never to do

- **Never heat your home with a gas oven or stove, a charcoal or gas grill, or a gas generator indoors.** They produce carbon monoxide, which is invisible, odorless and deadly.
- **Fuel-burning heaters** must be rated for indoor use and used exactly as the manufacturer instructs, with a working **carbon monoxide alarm**.
- **Keep space heaters at least 3 feet** from anything that can burn, and plug them **directly into the power station**, not into a power strip.
