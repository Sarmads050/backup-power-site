Pairing a diesel heater with a battery is one of the smartest ways to stay warm off-grid: the diesel makes the heat, and the battery only runs the fan, pump and controls. Choosing a power station for a diesel heater is mostly about one thing people overlook — the start-up surge.

## How much power a diesel heater uses

Diesel air heaters (the 2–8 kW units popular in vans, campers, workshops and tents) use electricity in two very different ways:

- **Start-up:** a glow plug heats up to ignite the fuel. Owners commonly report around **19–21 A at 12 V (roughly 230–250 W)** for a short time — from under a minute to a few minutes, depending on the model.
- **Running:** once lit, the glow plug switches off and the heater only runs a fan and fuel pump: roughly **0.7–3 A (about 8–36 W)**, depending on the heat setting.
- **Shut-down:** the fan keeps running for a few minutes to cool the burner, so never cut the power to stop it.

## Nightly energy use

A typical night on a medium setting:

- Running: about **25 W × 8 hours = 200 Wh**
- One start-up: about **240 W × 3 minutes ≈ 12 Wh**

That's roughly **210–250 Wh a night**. Higher heat settings and multiple restarts push it up; a low setting brings it down.

## How long a power station will run a diesel heater

On a 12V DC port (about 90% efficiency), for about 220 Wh per night:

| Power station | Approximate nights |
|---|---|
| 300 Wh | about 1 night |
| 500 Wh | about 2 nights |
| 1,000 Wh | about 4 nights |

Compare that with an electric space heater, which can drain a 1,000 Wh battery in about half an hour.

## The port matters more than the battery size

This is where most problems happen. Many power stations' standard **12V car ports are limited to around 10 A**. A diesel heater's **~20 A start-up spike** can exceed that, so the power station cuts out, the heater fails to start, or it throws a low-voltage error.

Look for:

- A **high-current 12V output** — often labeled for RV use and rated around **25–30 A**.
- A short, **thick cable** and solid connectors. Long, thin wires drop voltage, and diesel heaters are sensitive to low voltage at start-up.
- Your heater's own **start-up current** in its manual, compared against the port's rating.

Running the heater from the AC outlet through a 12V power adapter is another option — but the adapter must handle the start-up current too, and you lose energy in the conversion.

## Safety first

- Install the heater with the **combustion air intake and exhaust outside**, sealed and routed exactly as the manufacturer instructs.
- Use a working **carbon monoxide alarm** wherever people sleep.
- Keep the **fuel tank and exhaust** away from flammable materials, and never block the exhaust.
- Don't cut power while the heater is running — let it complete its cool-down cycle.
