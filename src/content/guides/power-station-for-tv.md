Watching TV during a power outage or at a campsite is one of the easiest jobs for a battery. Choosing a power station to run a TV is mostly about how many hours you want to watch — almost any unit has enough output.

## How many watts a TV uses

Modern LED and LCD TVs are far more efficient than older plasma sets. Typical figures:

| Screen size | Typical power use | 1,000 Wh runtime (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| 32-inch | about 30–50 W | about 17–28 hours |
| 43–50-inch | about 50–90 W | about 9–17 hours |
| 55-inch | about 60–120 W | about 7–14 hours |
| 65-inch | about 100–150 W+ | about 6–8 hours |

Runtime is roughly *capacity × 0.85 ÷ TV watts*. The 0.85 accounts for energy lost in the power station's inverter.

**Picture settings matter a lot.** Bright "vivid" modes and HDR can push power use well above the typical figure, while an energy-saving mode can cut it noticeably. The rated power on your TV's back label is the upper end; your real use is usually lower.

## How long common sizes last

For a typical 55-inch TV using about 100 W:

- **300 Wh:** about 2.5 hours — a movie or two.
- **500 Wh:** about 4 hours.
- **1,000 Wh:** about 8.5 hours.
- **2,000 Wh:** about 17 hours.

## Don't forget the extras

A TV rarely runs alone. Add:

- **Streaming stick or box:** about 3–10 W.
- **Soundbar:** about 15–40 W.
- **Wi-Fi router and modem:** about 20 W.
- **Game console:** often 100–200 W while playing.

A 55-inch TV plus router and streaming stick is about **125 W**, so a 1,000 Wh power station gives roughly **7 hours** of evening entertainment.

## What to look for in the power station

- **Pure sine wave AC output** — standard on modern power stations and best for electronics.
- **Continuous output above your total watts** — usually easy; most units offer 300 W or more.
- **Enough Wh for your viewing time** — use the table above, or plan around 100 Wh per hour for a mid-size TV.

## Watch longer on the same battery

- **Lower the brightness** and use an eco or standard picture mode.
- **Switch the AC outlet off** when you're done — the inverter uses power even with nothing plugged in.
- **Use a smaller screen** for outages; a laptop or tablet uses far less.
- **Recharge with solar** during the day for evening viewing.
