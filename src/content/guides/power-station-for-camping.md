A power station for camping replaces the noise and fumes of a generator with silent, plug-and-play power for your phones, lights, fridge and more. The right size depends on what you bring and how long you stay. Here's how to work it out.

## Start with what you'll actually power

Here's a realistic day for a car-camping setup:

| Device | Power | Hours/day | Energy/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12V compressor fridge | averages about 15 W | 24 | about 360 Wh |
| LED camp lights (2) | 10 W each | 5 | 100 Wh |
| Phones (2) | 15 W each | 2 | 60 Wh |
| Fan | 15 W | 8 | 120 Wh |
| Starlink Mini | about 40 W | 6 | 240 Wh |
| **Total** | | | **about 880 Wh/day** |

Your list will differ — skip Starlink and the total drops to about 640 Wh; add a CPAP and it rises by 300–700 Wh a night.

## Size by trip type

After about 15% conversion loss and a 20% safety buffer:

| Trip | Typical setup | Suggested size |
|---|---|---|
| Overnight, basics | Phones, lights, speaker | 300–500 Wh |
| Weekend with fridge | 12V fridge, lights, phones, fan | 1,000–1,500 Wh |
| Weekend, fully loaded | Fridge, Starlink, CPAP, laptops | 2,000–3,000 Wh |
| A week or longer | Any of the above | 1,000–2,000 Wh **plus 200–400 W of solar** |

For example, the 880 Wh/day setup above needs about **2,500 Wh for a two-day weekend** without solar — or a **1,000–1,500 Wh unit with a 200 W panel** that refills most of the day's use.

## Solar: the key to longer trips

Solar turns a weekend battery into a week-long one. A **200 W panel** in about 5 good sun hours puts back roughly **750 Wh a day** after real-world losses. Angle panels toward the sun, move them as shadows shift, and keep them out of the shade. Our solar charging time calculator shows how fast your panels refill your battery.

## DC vs AC: save 10–15%

Power DC devices from DC ports whenever you can:

- **12V fridge, diesel heater fan, Starlink Mini:** use the 12V or USB-C ports.
- **Phones, tablets, laptops:** use USB-C — it's more efficient than plugging the charger into the AC outlet.
- **Turn off the AC inverter** when nothing needs it; it draws power even when idle.

## Watch out for big appliances

Coffee makers, kettles, microwaves, hair dryers and electric grills draw **900–1,500 W or more**. They need a power station with high continuous output (often 1,800 W+) and they drain batteries fast. Many campers use a gas stove outdoors for cooking and save the battery for the fridge and electronics.

## Practical camping tips

- **Charge fully at home** and pre-cool your fridge before you leave.
- **Keep the power station shaded** and ventilated; heat shortens battery life.
- **In cold weather**, keep it in the tent or vehicle overnight and don't charge below freezing.
- **Check campground rules** — some limit generators but allow silent power stations.
