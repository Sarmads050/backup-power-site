A power station for a 12V fridge is the backbone of most camping, overlanding and van setups. The good news: modern 12V compressor fridges are efficient, and because they run on DC power, a power station can run one for days.

## How much power a 12V fridge uses

A 12V compressor fridge (the kind sold as a portable fridge or fridge-freezer) works like your home fridge: the compressor switches on and off to hold the temperature.

- **While running:** most draw about **1–6 amps** at 12 V — around **2.5 A (30 W)** is typical.
- **Compressor running time:** often about **half the time**, more in hot weather.
- **Daily energy:** about **30 amp-hours**, or roughly **360 Wh a day**, in mild conditions. In hot weather or with frequent opening, it can climb toward **700 Wh**.

Convert amps to watts by multiplying by 12 for a 12V fridge: 2.5 A × 12 V = 30 W.

## Use the 12V port, not the AC outlet

This is the biggest single tip. A power station stores energy as DC. Plugging a 12V fridge into the **12V car-style port** sends power almost directly. Using the **AC outlet** with an adapter converts DC to AC and back to DC — wasting energy twice and keeping the inverter running all day.

We assume about 90% efficiency on the 12V port and 85% or less on AC.

## How long a power station will run a 12V fridge

For a fridge averaging about 15 W across the day (360 Wh/day), on the 12V port:

| Power station | Mild weather (≈360 Wh/day) | Hot weather (≈700 Wh/day) |
|---|---|---|
| 300 Wh | about 18 hours | about 9 hours |
| 500 Wh | about 30 hours | about 15 hours |
| 1,000 Wh | about 2.5 days | about 1.3 days |
| 2,000 Wh | about 5 days | about 2.5 days |

## What size to buy

- **Weekend trip without solar:** about **1,000 Wh** gives a comfortable margin for the fridge plus phones and lights.
- **Fridge only, one night:** a **300–500 Wh** unit works in mild weather.
- **Week-long trips:** pair a **500–1,000 Wh** unit with **100–200 W of solar**. A 200 W panel in 5 good sun hours puts back about 750 Wh — more than a 12V fridge uses in most conditions.

## Ways to cut your fridge's energy use

- **Pre-cool at home** on mains power before you leave, and load it with already-cold food and drinks.
- **Keep it in the shade** and leave space around the vents.
- **Open it less often** and close the lid quickly.
- **Use eco mode** if your fridge has one, and set it no colder than you need (around 37–40°F for a fridge).
- **Avoid thermoelectric coolers** for long trips — they run constantly and can use several times more energy.

## Check the 12V port rating

Most power stations' 12V car ports are rated around **10 A (120 W)**, which is plenty for a 12V fridge. Use a good-quality cable and plug; loose car-port plugs are a common cause of a fridge shutting off overnight.
